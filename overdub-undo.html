Overdub & undo

Try a second part over a playing loop. Step back through that take or return to an earlier version of the selected slot.

Overdub a part while the loop plays

  1. Select the track that is playing your recorded loop. Confirm its TRACK and SLOT numbers on the performance screen.
  2. Tap REC once. The loop keeps playing and ODUB appears on the OLED. Play a new part over it. You can add more notes each time the loop repeats.
  3. Tap REC again to finish the overdub, even halfway through a bar. You hear the new notes on each repeat. The loop stays the same length.

If the track is stopped, tapping REC on a slot that contains a loop starts playback first. Tap it again while the loop plays to overdub. A long press of REC clears the selected slot.

Hear the loop with and without your change

  • Double-tap REC on the selected slot to undo its most recent change. After finishing an overdub, you’ll hear the loop as it sounded before you added the new part.
  • Triple-tap REC to bring the overdub back. Make the three taps in quick succession. If you make a new change to the slot after undoing, its earlier redos are discarded.
  • Read the undo count. UNDO shows how many steps you can undo on the selected slot. ODUB counts undo steps in the current overdub, and EDIT counts steps in the current note edit.

During an overdub, each completed pass around the loop that adds material can be undone separately. After you finish the take, one undo removes the whole overdub. While NOTE_EDIT is open, double-tap REC to undo your last note edit, or triple-tap to redo it. After you leave the editor, one undo brings the loop back to the way it sounded before that visit.

Choose the slot you mean to change

Undo follows the selected slot. For example, to undo a bass overdub recorded on track 1, slot 4, select track 1 and its fourth slot first. After copying a loop, undo the paste on the slot you copied it to. After clearing a slot, double-tap REC there to restore the loop if that undo step is still available.

Reach an older step on the card

Each slot keeps up to 99 versions of its loop, counting the current version and those available on the card. The looper holds 99 versions in memory for all tracks combined. With card saving enabled, it can recall older versions from the card as well. The UNDO count includes the steps you can reach in both places. When a slot reaches its limit, its oldest version gives way to the next change.

Double-tap REC as usual. If the looper needs an older take from the card, the current loop keeps playing until that version is ready. If you ask for several more undo steps while it loads, playback switches once to the last version you chose.

If memory fills before the card has saved a change, that undo step can be lost. If the looper cannot read the older version or has too little memory to play it, the current loop keeps playing. Clearing the slot or loading a set cancels the recall.

Which undo steps come back? Reopening the autosaved working set after power-up restores recent versions and older steps saved on the card. Loading a milestone restores the arrangement and whichever steps were in memory when you saved it. A milestone leaves out versions that were already held only on the card.
