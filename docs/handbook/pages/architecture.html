The components

  • Inputs. The DROID controller sends its buttons and faders on MIDI channels 13–16, which are never recorded. The computer and a DIN interface send notes and MIDI clock. USB serial carries !ENGINE commands from the host scripts.
  • Firmware modules. ControlSurface turns the DROID's messages and the encoder into gestures and windows. MidiInput sends the clock to the engine's clock and notes to the engine at the last played tick. SerialCommands is the console for diagnostics and tests. The library, the saved selection, the device settings, the encoder, the screen view and the boot health check sit beside them.
  • The engine. Gestures write a slot's states: a take, an overdub wrap, an edit or an undo. Composition jobs turn a state into windows of playback ahead of time, in steps of at most 810 µs. Playback plays those windows tick by tick. Storage saves every slot and the milestones to the card in the background.
  • Outputs. Playback goes to DIN and the computer; the LEDs, fader bank and motor positions go to the DROID; the OLED draws the piano roll and status band; the SD card holds the store and the library.

The firmware's main loop calls these parts in nine stages each pass: input, serial commands, due ticks, background work, output, the control surface, feedback, the screen and a health check.

A note's path

  1. MidiInput reads the note from DIN, USB or the DROID's USB host port and hands it to the engine.
  2. CaptureWriter writes it into the take, reserving room for its note-off at the same time.
  3. SlotTransport publishes the slot's new state when the loop wraps. That state is one undo step.
  4. A composer job merges the slot's takes into the next window of playback, a few steps at a time, ahead of the playhead.
  5. PlaybackTrack plays the composed window at its ticks.
  6. MidiPorts sends the note to DIN and USB.

Nothing is composed while a tick plays. Work that grows with a loop's layers is cut into short steps, and a tick that comes due between two steps plays first, which keeps the timing steady even with many overdubs.
