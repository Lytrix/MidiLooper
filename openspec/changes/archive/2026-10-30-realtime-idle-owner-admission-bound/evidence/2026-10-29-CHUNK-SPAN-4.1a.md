# Task 4.1a — chunk span is a conservative content extent (storage-owned)

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Change:** `realtime-idle-owner-admission-bound`
**Owner:** `LoopEventStore` (chunk metadata) + `CommittedEventRange` (admission reader)
**Status:** implemented, native verified, **not wired** to the overdub source view yet.

## The contract this establishes

> A chunk's advertised span is a **conservative superset** of its contained event ticks. Window
> admission may return extra events, but must never exclude a chunk containing an event in the
> requested interval.

`inWindow` over-admission costs a copy; under-admission loses content. Before this slice the metadata
was the *append-order endpoint pair*, which is not a bound at all once a capture pass wraps.

## Why `firstTick`/`lastTick` could not simply be redefined

They are **overloaded**:
- `lastAppendedTick_ = chunk(chunkIds_.back()).lastTick` (4 sites) — chronology.
- `lowerBoundIndex`: `if (c.lastTick < tick)` — assumes a sorted chunk, i.e. `lastTick == max`.

So the endpoints are preserved untouched and a new pair was added:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `firstTick` / `lastTick` | append-order endpoints (unchanged; used for chronology) |
| **`minTick` / `maxTick`** (new) | conservative content extent; `chunkTickSpan` returns these |

The struct documents that a wrap-spanning chunk deliberately advertises nearly the whole loop, so a
later reader cannot "optimise" it back to endpoint semantics and recreate the defect.

## Where the extent is maintained

- `appendToTailChunk` — first event initialises both; every later append is `min`/`max` compared.
  **This is the site that fixes the bug** (out-of-order appends: a capture wrap, a reconciled rewrite).
- Every site that rewrites or trims chunk content sets the extent alongside the endpoints, from the
  ordered events: private-filter refresh, canonical trim, ordered replacement, and the three
  sort/filter rewrites. `allocChunk`/`tryFreeChunk` zero it.

## `cer_win` cost measurement (required before wiring)

Dense, wrap-heavy pass — 31 bars, 10 notes/bar, 622 events in **3 chunks** (play order with a wrap
close), a 16-bar window and a 4-bar window at bar 8:

| window | chunks copied | events appended |
|---|---|---|
| 4 bars | 2 of 3 | **80** of 622 |
| 16 bars | **3 of 3** | **320** of 622 |

**This is the result that matters for the design:** at 31 bars and this density a 256-event chunk spans
~10 bars, so a **16-bar window admits every chunk**. The conservative bound therefore does not make the
16-bar derive cheaper than the whole-pass derive in *copy* terms; it only halves the reconstruction
(320 notes vs ~620). A 4-bar window does filter the copy as well.

**Consequence:** the source-view bound must be a **sub-window grain** (task 4.5), not "the 16-bar
window". Windowing alone does not remove the 201 ms `lcr,pairrows,at=passes,count=610` span — it roughly
halves it. The per-turn owner budget plus resumable sub-windows are what bound it, and the device oracle
is what proves it.
